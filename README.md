# Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) & RFM Analysis

## Project Overview

This project analyzes retail transaction data to understand customer purchasing behavior, segment customers based on their transaction history, and evaluate their historical profitability.

Using Python and Pandas, the analysis combines Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA), Recency-Frequency-Monetary (RFM) segmentation, historical Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) analysis, and customer strategy grouping.

The goal is to identify valuable customer segments, recognize customers who may be at risk of becoming inactive, and develop data-driven strategies for retention, reactivation, and revenue growth.

## Business Objectives

- Analyze sales, profit, product, store, and regional performance.
- Understand customer purchasing behavior using RFM metrics.
- Segment customers according to recency, frequency, and monetary value.
- Calculate historical customer revenue, cost, and profit.
- Classify customers into low, medium, and high historical CLV segments.
- Combine RFM segments with CLV segments to identify strategic customer groups.
- Develop recommendations for customer retention, win-back campaigns, and cross-selling.

## Methodology

### 1. Data Cleaning and Preparation

The analysis includes:

- Inspecting dataset structure, data types, and missing values.
- Checking duplicate records and unusual transaction values.
- Calculating profit as sales amount minus cost.
- Removing records with non-positive quantities or sales amounts.
- Preparing a cleaned dataset for EDA and customer-level analysis.

The notebook reports no missing values or exact duplicate rows in the original transaction data.

### 2. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

The project examines:

- Sales amount distribution.
- Monthly sales trends.
- Sales by product, region, store, and category.
- Quantity sold by product.
- Profit by product.
- Customer transaction distribution by gender.
- Sales versus profit relationships.
- Top 10 customers by total sales.

These analyses provide an overview of sales performance and customer purchasing patterns.

### 3. RFM Analysis

RFM analysis evaluates customer behavior through three metrics:

- **Recency:** Number of days since the customer's most recent transaction.
- **Frequency:** Number of recorded transaction rows associated with the customer.
- **Monetary:** Total sales amount generated by the customer.

The analysis date is set to one day after the latest transaction date.

Customers are assigned scores from 1 to 5 for each RFM dimension, then grouped into the following segments:

- Champions
- Loyal Customers
- Potential Loyalists
- At Risk
- Lost Customers
- Need Attention

### 4. Historical Customer Lifetime Value

The notebook calculates customer-level metrics including:

- Total revenue
- Total cost
- Total profit
- Total quantity
- Transaction count
- First purchase date
- Last purchase date
- Customer lifetime in observed days
- Average purchase value

The notebook defines historical CLV as total recorded customer profit.

**Important:** This is a historical value measure, not a predictive estimate of future customer lifetime value.

### 5. CLV Segmentation

Customers are classified into three groups based on their historical CLV ranking:

- Low CLV
- Medium CLV
- High CLV

The notebook uses three quantile-based groups to create a relative segmentation of customers.

### 6. RFM and CLV Integration

The RFM and CLV datasets are combined to analyze customer value across behavioral segments.

The analysis includes:

- Customer counts by RFM and CLV segment.
- Total and average CLV by RFM segment.
- Identification of high-CLV customers.
- Identification of high-value customers classified as At Risk.
- Identification of Champions with High CLV.
- A combined customer strategy grouping.

### 7. Customer Strategy Groups

The notebook assigns customers to strategic groups based on RFM and CLV classifications.

| Strategy Group | Intended Objective |
|---|---|
| VIP / Retain | Retain Champions with High CLV |
| Win Back | Re-engage high-CLV customers classified as At Risk |
| Upsell / Cross-sell | Develop additional value from eligible Loyal Customers |
| Develop | Encourage Potential Loyalists to increase engagement |
| Reactivate | Attempt to re-engage Lost Customers |
| Monitor | Monitor customers assigned to other segment combinations |

These groups provide a framework for prioritizing customer engagement activities.

## Business Recommendations

### 1. Retain High-Value Customers

Prioritize personalized offers and loyalty initiatives for customers classified as Champions with High CLV.

### 2. Re-engage High-Value Customers at Risk

Identify customers with High CLV who are classified as At Risk and investigate suitable win-back campaigns.

### 3. Develop Loyal Customers

Explore relevant cross-selling and upselling opportunities for Loyal Customers with medium or high CLV.

### 4. Improve Engagement Among Potential Loyalists

Encourage repeat purchases through targeted recommendations, relevant promotions, and customer engagement initiatives.

### 5. Reactivate Lost Customers

Evaluate reactivation campaigns for Lost Customers while considering their historical spending and profitability.

### 6. Monitor Segment Performance

Track customer counts, historical profit, purchase frequency, and segment migration over time to evaluate the performance of customer strategies.

These recommendations should be tested and monitored before their business impact is assumed.

## Limitations

- CLV is calculated as historical total profit and does not predict future customer value.
- Frequency counts transaction rows; the source data's transaction identifier and grain should be verified before interpreting it as the number of distinct orders.
- RFM scores are relative to the observed customer distribution.
- CLV segments are based on quantile ranking, so they represent relative groups rather than fixed monetary thresholds.
- The observation period is approximately 19 months, which may not represent customers' full lifetime.
- Customer strategy assignments are rule-based and should be evaluated against actual campaign outcomes.
- The notebook contains repeated analysis cells that could be consolidated for better maintainability.
